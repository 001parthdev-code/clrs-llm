31.5 The Chinese remainder theorem

Around 100 C.E., the Chinese mathematician Sun-Ts solved the

problem of finding those integers x that leave remainders 2, 3, and 2

when divided by 3, 5, and 7 respectively. One such solution is x = 23,

and all solutions are of the form 23+105k for arbitrary integers k. The

"Chinese remainder theorem" provides a correspondence between a

system of equations modulo a set of pairwise relatively prime moduli

(for example, 3, 5, and 7) and an equation modulo their product (for

example, 105).

The Chinese remainder theorem has two major applications. Let the

integer n be factored as n = n1n2 nk, where the factors ni are

pairwise relatively prime. First, the Chinese remainder theorem is a

descriptive "structure theorem" that describes the structure of n as

identical to that of the Cartesian product with

componentwise addition and multiplication modulo ni in the ith

component. Second, this description helps in designing efficient

algorithms, since working in each of the systems can be more

efficient (in terms of bit operations) than working modulo n.

x = a (mod ni)

As an example of the application of the Chinese remainder theorem,
suppose that you are given the two equations

a = 2 (mod 5),
a = 3 (mod 13),

so that a1 = 2, n1 = m2 = 5, a2 = 3, and n2 = m1 = 13, and you wish to
compute a mod 65, since n = n1n2 = 65. Because 13-1 = 2 (mod 5) and

5-1 = 8 (mod 13), you compute

c1 = 13 · (2 mod 5) = 26,
c2 = 5 · (8 mod 13) = 40,

and

a = 2 · 26 + 3 · 40 (mod 65)

= 52 + 120 (mod 65)

= 42 (mod 65).
